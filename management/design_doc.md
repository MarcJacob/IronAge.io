# IRONAGE.IO DESIGN DOCUMENT - Intro

Openfront-like lightweight game themed on medieval / feudal strategy & diplomacy and subjugating other players as an alternative to conquest,
with multiple paths to any sort of victory the player is aiming for.

The map is built in an Openfront-like way with the use of texelized terrain that is freely conquerable.

The Game focuses on economic buildup in cooperation with other players inevitably leading to inequalities and imbalances, creating conflict. The conflict leads to the creation of vassal chains and internal country politics between players, until the game ends due to one power taking control, or a player manages to become rich enough.

# Alpha (First playable version, reduced feature set, smaller maps & shorter games)

Alpha version is a version of the game's design that is meant to be reduced in scope, but no less coherent.

Focus: Tight game loop based on a simple economic buildup mechanic (trade -> gold -> buildings to increase population / train troops -> raise armies). The goal of the economy is to strike the right balance between attracting trade and building larger population centers.

## Game Elements

### Settlements

Center of population owned by a player, holding territory.

#### Population

Settlement population follows a logarithmic growth curve following a target population / "occupations".
Target population comes from multiple sources, primarily land and buildings.
The growth curve actually makes the population stabilize slightly above the target, naturally creating "pressure".

#### Land exploitation / colonization + proximity & contestation mechanic

When a settlement has positive population pressure, it will expand into nearby empty tiles in order to create require occupations.
If no empty tile is available, it can start pushing into the territory of other settlements, if it has enough pressure compared to them.

How much population a tile allows the settlement to support / how much the target population increases is a function of the tile's output (based on terrain) + a bonus related to proximity.

Proximity is a value determining how close to the settlement a tile on its territory is. It is reduced by total travel cost from the tile to the settlement.
At 0 proximity, the tile cannot be claimed by the settlement no matter what.

Travel cost is based on terrain type, or the presence of a road.

#### Local Wealth & Construction

Settlements have *local wealth* which can be periodically taxed into a separate money pool called the *treasury* (in alpha, one pool tied to the owner).

Local wealth is generated by:
- Land output.
- Buildings.
- Caravans reaching the settlement / caravans originating from the settlement reaching other places.

Treasury can be used to:
- Raise armies and pay their upkeep.
- Build defensive buildings.
- Transfer to local wealth.

Local wealth being accumulated in a settlement has positive effects, capped at a certain fraction of the population:
- Increased attractivity for trade.
- Increased base trade income per caravan.
- Increased caravan spawn chance.

Thoses bonuses depend on local wealth / population fraction, going from -50% at 0, to +100% at x2. From there the bonus keeps increasing at a slower and slower pace.

To grow a settlement it is wise to strike a balance between accumulating local wealth, limiting taxation into treasury and building upgrades made to attract / emit trade and increase population cap.

Local wealth is spent by the settlement in the following ways:
- Base cost per population (subtracted from some income sources).
- Construction financing. (No more wealth = construction suspended).
- Local troops upkeep. (No more wealth = troops can't be raised and start disbanding).

##### Buildings

Some buildings that can be built in settlements, with their effect and construction time & cost: 
- Housing (Pop capacity +100), 100 ticks, 100 wealth
- Caravanserai (+1 max caravan), 400 ticks, 500 wealth
- Barracks (Men At Arms local capacity +10, Horsemen +5, +1 recruitment every 100 ticks), 500 ticks, 500 wealth
- Keep (+25% defense bonus, Knights local capacity +5), 2500 ticks, 2000 wealth, max 4 levels
- Walls (+50% defense bonus, +10% trade attraction), 2000 ticks, 1000 wealth, max 4 levels
- Trade district (+10% trade income, +25% trade attraction), 1000 ticks, 1000 wealth, max 10 levels.

Those are purely given as examples. If no max level is indicated, they can be stacked infinitely.

### Trade

Caravans / trading fleets are generated randomly as a function of population, local wealth, upgrades... They target another village at random using the trade network as an itinerary (roads on land, established sea trade lanes on water). They compute a value according to how much they will make getting to their destination.
On every stop until reaching the target, they generate local wealth for both their home village and the village they stopped at, as a % of their value.
When they reach their target, they disappear and transfer their value both to the target and their origin village.

Caravans can only travel on roads. Linking settlements by road adds them to a common "road network" caravans use to determine their path.

#### Target selection

Which target settlement a newly spawned caravan / trade fleet will choose is based on:
- Travel time from home village (so fleets will usually be much better at going further away).
- Local wealth in target.
- Upgrades in target.
- Population at target.

Every candidate outputs a score, and a random one is chosen weighted on that score.

### War & conquest

Armies can be raised at any owned settlement from local population & local troops, with four troop types:
- Levies, immediately raised from the local population at no immediate cost. Low upkeep.
- Men-at-arms, medium strength and upkeep. Bonus damage against horsemen.
- Horsemen, medium strength and upkeep. Bonus damage against levies. High speed if the army is only made up of horsemen / knights.
- Knights, very high strength and upkeep. Bonus damage against everything other than other knights. High speed if the army is only made up of horsemen / knights.

Armies move around the map freely, go faster on roads. Their total upkeep cost is slightly superlinear with total troop count, encouraging quality over quantity when possible for longer use but not to the point it's better to field one knight over 25 levies (since being outnumbered is a penalty in itself).

Hostile armies fight one another automatically when coming into contact, neutral armies can be told to fight manually. 

When a battle begins, the armies are pinned in place and start inflicting damage on one another, with base damage depending on a simple sum based on troop counts & types, and bonus damage coming from bonuses like outnumbering the enemy and troop types countering eachother. Each army takes the damage inflicted to their side as a function of their troop count compared to total troop count on their side. The damage is then further spread on each troop type. Each troop type has a defense value which comes up to a total ssum which deducts from the damage value randomly from a quarter to all of the value (ex: knights have 4 defense, 100 knights take 200 damage, they have a total of 400 defense. Random roll gives 300, deducted from 200 = no damage taken).

Armies can join an ongoing battle at any time by "attacking" the side they want to fight against. Battles are never three-way, all armies are on one side or another.

When an army attacks a settlement, a siege begins. The besieger can choose to wait and starve the settlement, reducing its population & local troops over time, or attack, starting a battle where the defenders have a bonus in attack & defense according to buildings (so they won't inflict much damage but will take a long time to kill and in total might inflict bad casualties).

Armies can cross water freely but have a very reduced speed and increased upkeep while doing so.
By default armies will use pathfinding using roads unless "direct movement" is used in which case they will go in a straight line.

Armies coming across a caravan / trade ship can plunder it, killing the caravan and giving its carried gold to the army owner's treasury.

Armies can be set to accompany friendly armies to automatically follow and help them in battle, effectively making them one unit.
Armies are bound to their origin settlement and CANNOT merge into others.

#### Troop recruitment & Raising

Armies can be raised next to any settlement. During the raising process, the owner sets a target count of each troop type. While raising, the army is immobile. Local troops move in / out quickly while levies are recruited "in place" and come gradually from the settlement population.

When given an order to move, the army will start moving there once it is done raising the required troop count.

A settlement can build upgrades allowing it to take from local wealth and / or treasury to periodically recruit soldiers into the local troops.
All available local troop types can be set to a ceiling where recruitment continues until reaching that ceiling.

Goal: Simplicity, center recruitment of high quality troops in specific places. Not every settlement should have recruitment buildings.

### Upkeep & Economy

Armies have an upkeep cost paid straight from the player's treasury.
Running out of gold means all armies lose some men relative to how far below 0 the player went.

## Diplomacy

Simple diplomacy system:
- Neutral = no automated actions between units. Trade is allowed. Doesn't change for "minor" offenses like pillaging a caravan, but automatically goes to war if on opposing sides of a battle.
- Alliance = Armies can accompany eachother and automatically help eachother in battle (nearby ally in battle against enemy / neutral force = auto move there and help if idle).
- War = Armies automatically engage eachother when nearby and idle. Caravans / trade ships never automatically choose an enemy target or paths that go through enemy lands.

Players can communicate using a simple chat system (temporary, will move to purely emoji and signals later).

## Winning the game

The goal of the game is to reach one of the victory conditions:
- Sovereign victory: Have at least 75% of the land controlled by you or your vassals.
- Development victory: Have at least 50% of the world population in directly owned settlements.
- Merchant victory: Have at least 50% of all world wealth in directly owned settlements.

When any of those victory conditions, the victory screen shows the victor and the players who were closest to getting the other victory conditions.

Every player is assigned a score based on various factors, some from the state of the game at the end (current population, military strength, wealth...) and others accumulated (buildings built, kills, conquered settlements...) + a multiplier for vassal players the longer they've been a vassal.
